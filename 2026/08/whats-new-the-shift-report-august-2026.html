What's New on The Shift Report: August 2026

The Shift Report · RPSGT · BRPT · CMS · Free CEC · AAST · sleep technologists

The Shift Report has a fresh group of updates for sleep technologists, RPSGT and CCSH credential holders, students, educators, and anyone trying to keep up with the professional side of sleep medicine without getting buried in noise.

We have also tightened the way The Shift Report is being produced. Current posts are being built around primary or authoritative sources, checked for what actually changed, reviewed by a human editor, and published selectively. The goal is simple: fewer recycled headlines and more practical information that a sleep technologist can actually use.

New: RPSGT Recertification in 2026

The newest credentialing editorial focuses on what RPSGT and CCSH credential holders should check before a recertification deadline. The article reviews current BRPT requirements, ISR credit limits, documentation, BLS/CPR expectations, timing, and why keeping your own CEC records matters.

The emphasis is practical: do not make the expiration date your quality-control check.

New: CMS Sleep Study Fact Check

One of the easiest ways to misread a Medicare page is to see a recent update date and assume coverage changed.

The Shift Report's new CMS fact-check shows why the revision history matters. A current sleep-study article received a 2026 update, but the cited revision was administrative and did not represent a new nationwide sleep-study coverage change.

That is exactly the kind of distinction we want The Shift Report to make before an update becomes a headline.

New: Free CEC Corner

The Free CEC Corner now includes four currently verified AAST opportunities for eligible members: two 2026 A2Zzz issues offering four AAST CECs each, plus two one-credit webinars.

We are being careful with the wording here. These are described as member-free when AAST lists a nonmember price. Readers should still verify current eligibility, cost, credit value, completion requirements, and BRPT recertification rules before enrolling.

Recent Editorial: When Software Scores the Night

The Shift Report also recently looked at the growing role of full-PSG autoscoring and what it means for the sleep technologist.

The central idea is not that software is good or bad. It is that automation makes human review, signal-quality awareness, scoring competency, and quality assurance even more important. A technologist still needs enough knowledge to recognize when an automated result does not fit the recording in front of them.

Why the Shift Report Is Changing

Sleep medicine produces more updates than any one technologist can reasonably follow. But more posts do not automatically mean more value.

The Shift Report is moving toward a slower, more deliberate model: verify the source, identify what is actually new, explain why it matters to the sleep technology community, and avoid turning every recall, policy-page update, or press release into an alarm.

What readers should expect: practical sleep-technology context, clearer sourcing, credentialing and professional updates, verified education opportunities, and fact checks when a headline could easily overstate what actually changed.
