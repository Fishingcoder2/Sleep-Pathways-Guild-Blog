Sleep Pathways Guild Back-to-School Guide 2026

Sleep technology education · ASTEP · STAR · BRPT · RPSGT · CPSGT · professional development

There is no single entry route into sleep technology. Some learners begin through a formal college program, while others enter through employer-supported clinical training combined with an approved education pathway. The right choice depends on your current experience, the credential you are pursuing, and the rules that apply in your state.

Important: Education, accreditation, BRPT exam eligibility, credentialing, and state licensure are related—but they are not the same thing. Before paying for a program, confirm that it matches the exact eligibility pathway you plan to use.

Credentialed & Ready for a Degree

Look for degree-completion or allied-health programs that recognize prior education and professional credentials and support leadership, education, management, or advanced clinical roles.

ASTEP: AASM Sleep Technology Education

ASTEP is the American Academy of Sleep Medicine's education program for sleep technologists. Current offerings include introductory sleep-medicine education and online self-study modules. Depending on the BRPT pathway used, ASTEP education can satisfy an education component, but it does not automatically replace every separate experience or eligibility requirement.

STAR: BRPT-Designated Alternative Education

STAR stands for Sleep Technology Approved Resource. BRPT uses STAR designation for alternative education programs that are not CAAHEP- or CoARC-accredited. STAR programs may be designated as Self-Study, Focused, or Focused 2, and the category matters because different BRPT exam pathways require different combinations.

Do not assume “STAR” means one specific format. Check the provider's current STAR category and compare it with the BRPT eligibility pathway you plan to use.

CAAHEP / CoARC Programs and BRPT Eligibility

BRPT does not accredit schools. CAAHEP and CoARC accredit qualifying polysomnography education programs or approved add-on programs. BRPT recognizes graduates of qualifying programs under specific credential eligibility pathways.

For the RPSGT examination, BRPT currently lists a CAAHEP/CoARC graduate route as Pathway 3. For the CPSGT examination, the CAAHEP/CoARC route is currently Pathway 2. Candidates should always use the current BRPT handbook and eligibility pages because pathway rules can change.

Programs & Degree Options to Explore

Oregon Tech — Polysomnographic Technology

Oregon Tech currently offers an online Polysomnographic Technology Certificate and an Associate of Applied Science. The university states that the PSG certificate is CAAHEP-accredited and is designed to prepare graduates for the RPSGT exam pathway.

Important distinction: Oregon Tech specifically notes that the certificate is CAAHEP-accredited; the PSG AAS itself is not.

UNC Charlotte — Neurodiagnostics & Sleep Science

UNC Charlotte resources continue to reference the Bachelor of Science in Neurodiagnostics and Sleep Science as a post-professional pathway and indicate that an associate degree and professional registration are required for admission.

However, the current College of Health and Human Services academic-program summary does not list NDSS among its bachelor’s programs. Prospective students should confirm current admissions availability directly with UNC Charlotte before making plans or paying fees.

World Sleep Academy

World Sleep Academy is a cohort-based educational program of World Sleep Society. It combines asynchronous online learning, live faculty engagement, discussion, and practical application for clinicians, researchers, technologists, and other healthcare professionals.

It is best viewed as sleep-medicine education and professional development. A learner pursuing BRPT credential eligibility should separately verify whether their chosen pathway requires ASTEP, STAR, CAAHEP/CoARC education, clinical experience, or another qualifying route.

Before You Enroll: The Sleep Pathways Checklist

  1. Name your goal. Are you pursuing CPSGT, RPSGT, a college degree, career advancement, or broader sleep-medicine education?
  2. Open the current credential handbook. Do not rely on an old blog post, social-media post, or outdated program page for eligibility.
  3. Identify the exact pathway. Note required education type, clinical experience, documentation, BLS/CPR, and timing limits.
  4. Verify accreditation or designation. Confirm CAAHEP/CoARC accreditation or the specific STAR category when applicable.
  5. Check state law. Credential eligibility does not automatically equal state licensure eligibility.
  6. Ask about clinical placement. Determine who secures the site and what happens if a placement is unavailable.
  7. Compare total cost. Include tuition, books, exams, background checks, immunizations, travel, clinical requirements, and membership fees.
  8. Save documentation. Keep transcripts, completion certificates, clinical-hour records, and current BLS/CPR documentation.

Final Thoughts

Sleep technology offers multiple entry and advancement routes, but the best pathway is the one that matches your actual credential requirements, clinical situation, state rules, and long-term goals. Use official sources first, ask direct questions before enrolling, and keep building both your technical knowledge and your professional network.
